A barbershop that kept its salon habits.

Rosa never understood why the two trades were meant to live on separate streets. So on Alder Street, they don’t.

The story

In 2014 the unit at 128 Alder Street was a failing photocopy shop with good light and a bad lease.

Rosa Delgado took it anyway. She had spent six years on a salon floor doing colour and long-hair work, then trained as a barber because the queue outside the shop across the road never got shorter. The plan was one chair. By the end of the first winter there were two, because the people coming in for a $15 buzz kept asking whether she also did their partner’s greys.

Ten years on there are four chairs, a razor bench, and a basin that gets more use than any of it. The trades still sit side by side. A fade at nine, a keratin treatment at ten, a kid’s first haircut at eleven with the whole family filming it.

The name is the shop in two words. Blade is the clipper and the straight razor — the precision half, the part that has to be exact or it shows. Bloom is what happens after, at the basin, in the mirror, on the walk home.

House rules

What we hold ourselves to.

Four things every barber here agrees to before they get a chair.

The quote is the price

What we say at the basin is what you pay at the counter. If a service turns out to need more than quoted, we stop and ask before we start.

Nobody gets rushed

Slots are booked long on purpose. A walk-in never bumps someone out of the chair, and a running-late appointment never means a shortcut on the cut in progress.

We will tell you no

If a reference photo will not work on your hair, or colour will damage it, you get an honest answer and an alternative — not a shrug and a charge.

Everyone sits in the same chairs

One price list for everyone. Same care for a $15 buzz as for a $60 package. Kids, seniors, first-timers, regulars — same room, same standard.

In the shop

Practical things worth knowing.

The small stuff people usually only find out on their third visit.

Is the shop accessible?

Step-free entrance from Alder Street, and the front two chairs have room for a wheelchair transfer or to work chairside. Tell us when you book and we will keep one clear.

Can I bring my kid while I get cut?

Yes. There is a bench by the window and a box of things to do. Book the last slot before lunch if you want the quietest room.

Do you do first haircuts?

Often. Bring a camera. We keep a clipping envelope behind the counter for the ones that matter.

Can I buy the products you use?

Yes, everything on the shelf is for sale at shop price. Ask what went on your hair and we will show you the tin.

Do you take tips?

Gratefully, never expected. Cash or on the card, both fine.

Ten years in. Still the same rule.

Nobody leaves the chair unhappy. Come find out whether we mean it.
